Yields cratered following the announcement while stock market futures rose, with the 10-year closing down 5.7 bps to 4.647% and the 30-year down 9 bps to 5.196%. Worth noting the context: the move reversed a Treasury market rout that had pushed long-dated yields to the highest since 2007. If yields drift back up, today's equity relief could reverse — this is the single most market-relevant, under-appreciated line item today.

Market experts pointed to a higher term premium and a changing Treasury buyer base, and in addition cited increased supply of corporate debt, specifically related to artificial intelligence. Under-covered but consequential: the AI capex boom is starting to show up as a macro rates pressure, not just an equity story.
  • China puts its rare-earth "arsenal" back in play; Nov. 10 export-control deadline looms. Recent moves include China planning to increase output in the world's largest rare earth deposit by 50%, Congo banning copper/cobalt concentrate exports, and the Trump admin blocking tungsten and battery-waste exports. The expanded controls matter enormously: though suspended for one year until November 2026, full implementation could put an estimated USD 6.5 trillion per year of downstream production outside China at risk across automotive, high-tech, defence and energy. Watch materials/defense names into the deadline.
  • mRNA validation could re-rate the whole neoantigen-vaccine space beyond melanoma. This is the first randomized Phase 3 clinical trial aimed at definitively proving the benefit of "neoantigen" vaccines. Similar approaches are being tested against lung, breast and pancreatic cancers — meaning today's read-through extends well past MRK/MRNA to the broader biotech runner universe.
  • New-wave nuclear keeps compounding quietly as an AI-power play. Under the radar this month: Applied Atomics secured exclusive rights to BWX Technologies' mPower SMR technology for commercial deployment in the US, Canada and elsewhere, and South Korea's HanmiGlobal is forming a development company to enter the US SMR market. The through-line — these innovations are being rapidly commercialized to meet explosive AI computing demands — keeps nuclear/uranium names a live thematic for runner hunting.
